Residential locksmith work covers everything from quick lockout calls to full home security overhauls. Most homeowners interact with a locksmith only a few times in their life, and each time may involve a different service category. This article is a complete overview of what residential locksmiths do, organized by situation.

## Lockout services

Emergency lockouts are the most common residential locksmith call. The customer is locked out of their home and needs to get back in. A residential locksmith can typically open a standard residential door without damage, with a service call running seventy-five to two hundred dollars depending on time of day and lock complexity.

Skilled locksmiths almost always use non-destructive entry — pick guns, manual picks, or bypass tools — to avoid damaging the lock. Drilling is reserved for rare situations where the lock cannot be opened any other way, and a competent locksmith will explain why before drilling.

## Rekeying

Rekeying changes the internal pins of an existing lock so old keys no longer work. This is the standard service for new homeowners, after lost keys, after relationship changes, after employee turnover for home offices, or any other situation where the existing key may be in unwanted hands.

Rekeying typically costs twenty to fifty dollars per lock plus a service call fee. The work takes five to ten minutes per lock and reuses the existing hardware.

## Lock installation and replacement

When existing locks need to be replaced — due to age, damage, security upgrades, or aesthetic changes — a locksmith handles both removing the old hardware and installing the new. The work usually involves three to five locks for a typical home.

Pricing depends entirely on the locks chosen. Builder-grade replacements run fifty to one hundred dollars per lock plus installation. Mid-grade replacements run one to two hundred. High-security or smart locks run two to five hundred. Total cost for a typical home re-lock with mid-grade hardware runs four hundred to a thousand dollars.

## High-security lock upgrades

Homeowners concerned about specific threats — break-in attempts, lock bumping, lock picking — can upgrade to high-security locks from manufacturers known for actual high-security construction. These locks resist common attack techniques significantly better than standard residential hardware.

A locksmith experienced with high-security locks can assess whether the upgrade makes sense for the specific situation and which manufacturer is appropriate. Cost is higher but performance differences are real.

## Smart lock installation and configuration

Smart lock installation involves both physical mounting and electronic configuration. A locksmith can handle the full installation, integrate the lock with the homeowner's network, set up user codes, and walk the homeowner through app features. This is particularly useful for first-time smart lock owners or for installations on doors that need modification.

Some homeowners install smart locks themselves; the work is straightforward on a standard door. A locksmith adds value when the door needs modification, when integration with existing security systems is required, or when the homeowner wants someone to handle the entire setup.

## Window and door hardware

Residential locksmiths handle hardware beyond just primary deadbolts. Window locks, sliding door locks, French door locks, basement door locks, garage entry door locks, gate locks, and patio door locks all fall within the residential locksmith scope. Many of these locks are commonly weak points in home security and benefit from professional review.

A whole-home security walkthrough — where the locksmith inspects every lock and identifies weak points — is a valuable service that many homeowners don't know to ask for. Cost is typically modest and results in a prioritized list of upgrades the homeowner can address over time.

## Safe installation and service

Many homeowners have a residential safe — for documents, jewelry, firearms, or general valuables. Residential locksmiths handle safe selection advice, installation (often bolting the safe to floor or wall), combination changes, and service when the safe malfunctions.

Safes vary widely in capability and price. Fire safes protect against fire but not theft; burglary safes protect against forced entry but not fire; combination safes protect against both. A locksmith can match the safe to the threat being protected against.

## Key duplication

Standard residential keys are duplicated at any hardware store inexpensively. A locksmith offers key duplication for restricted or high-security keys that hardware stores can't handle, key duplication for older or unusual locks, and key cutting from existing locks when no key exists.

Most homeowners only need a locksmith for key duplication when the lock is something other than standard hardware-store fare.

## Mailbox and postal locks

Mailbox locks fall in a regulatory gray area — some are the homeowner's responsibility, some belong to the postal service. A residential locksmith can typically handle mailbox locks where the homeowner has authority to change them, and can advise when the postal service needs to be involved.

## Storm door, screen door, and security door locks

Secondary doors — storm doors, screen doors, security doors — have their own locks that often get neglected. A residential locksmith can handle installation, repair, and rekeying of these doors as part of broader home security work.

## Eviction and post-tenant rekeying

Rental property owners regularly need rekeying between tenants. A residential locksmith with experience in rental properties can handle the work efficiently, often with key control measures (numbered keys, written acknowledgment) that protect the property owner.

## Alarm system integration

Many residential alarm systems include lock control or integration. A locksmith can install hardware that integrates with the alarm system — locks that can be controlled from the alarm panel, locks that automatically engage when the alarm is armed, or locks that send signals when manipulated.

## Whole-home security assessments

Beyond fixing specific issues, a residential locksmith can assess the overall security of a home and identify priorities. The walkthrough covers every entry point, every lock, every window, and every weakness an attacker might exploit. The result is a prioritized list of upgrades the homeowner can address based on budget and concern.

This service is particularly valuable for new homeowners, after a break-in, or for homeowners who haven't reviewed their security in many years. The cost is modest compared to the value of having a professional eye on the property.

## Choosing a residential locksmith

The right residential locksmith has demonstrated residential experience (not just commercial or automotive), knowledge of common residential brands and hardware, a service area that covers the home's location, transparent pricing, and verifiable reviews. Establishing a relationship before an emergency means the right locksmith is on call when something happens, instead of the first Google result.

Once that relationship exists, the breadth of services a residential locksmith provides becomes more useful over years. Routine work like maintenance, occasional rekeying, key duplication for unusual locks, and security advice are all easier with a known locksmith than with a different stranger each time.
